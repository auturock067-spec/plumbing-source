Brass threaded elbow

Match the thread specification, not just the nominal size or appearance.

A threaded elbow changes the direction of a pipe connection. Brass appearance does not establish the alloy, service suitability or thread standard. Identify the connection at each end and request the manufacturer's dimensional drawing before specifying a mating component.

01 / SPECIFY

What to specify

These are fields to confirm, not supplied model ratings. Exact values depend on the selected product and service.

Thread standardRecord the exact standard and thread form for each end; a shared size label is not enough.
End configurationSpecify male/female ends, equal/reducing sizes and required elbow angle.
Material identityRequest alloy designation and supporting material documentation where required.
DimensionsCheck overall envelope, wrench flats and available assembly space against a drawing.
Sealing and serviceConfirm the specified sealing method, medium and model-specific pressure/temperature limits.
02 / CHECK

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Assuming BSP and NPT can be interchanged because their labels look similar.
  • Treating nominal pipe size as a measured outside diameter.
  • Using a material or pressure rating from a different fitting or alloy.
03 / ASK

Questions for a manufacturer

  1. What exact thread standard and end configuration will be supplied?
  2. Can you provide a dimensioned drawing and material designation?
  3. Which sealing and assembly instructions apply?
  4. What evidence supports suitability for our stated medium and conditions?

Do not confuse it with…

A threaded elbow is different from a compression elbow. Match the connection system and mating component before comparing suppliers or prices.
